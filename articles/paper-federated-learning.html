Federated learning in healthcare: Addressing AI challenges and operational realities under the GDPR, Journal of Data Protection & Privacy, vol. 7, no. 3

A paper in the Journal of Data Protection & Privacy on federated learning in healthcare under the GDPR

The paper

One of my publications is about federated learning in healthcare. Its title is "Federated learning in healthcare: Addressing AI challenges and operational realities under the GDPR", and it appears in the Journal of Data Protection & Privacy, volume 7, number 3 (Spring 2025), written with two colleagues from Dedalus.

The idea behind the topic

In plain words, federated learning is a way to train a shared model while the data stays with the organisations that hold it. Each one trains on its own data, and what gets shared are updates to the model, while the original records stay where they are.

It is easy to see why this is interesting for healthcare. Health data is sensitive, and in the European Union the GDPR sets rules for how personal data is handled. Keeping records in place can help, but it does not by itself settle every question about data protection.

Two sides of one question

The title puts two things next to each other: the challenges on the AI side and the operational realities under the GDPR. I find that a useful way to frame the subject. A method also has to work for the people and organisations who run it, within the rules that apply to them.
