Advancing LOINC Mapping with AI: Insights from LARA's RAG Implementation and Expert Evaluation, book chapter

A 2025 book chapter on an AI assistant for mapping local lab test names to LOINC, and on its evaluation by experts

The problem with lab test names

Clinical labs often have their own names for the tests they run. Those names make sense locally, but to speak a common language they need to be mapped to LOINC, a standard set of codes for identifying tests. Doing that mapping by hand is slow, and it is easy to make mistakes.

This is the problem behind LARA, the LOINC AI-based Mapping and Remapping Assistant. I built LARA with CNR-IIT. In 2025 it was the subject of a book chapter titled "Advancing LOINC Mapping with AI: Insights from LARA's RAG Implementation and Expert Evaluation".

An assistant for the experts

LARA takes a local lab test name and suggests LOINC codes for it, and it works with more than one language. The people doing the mapping review the suggestions and make the decision. The assistant is there to help with their work, while the judgement stays with them.

The "RAG" in the title stands for retrieval-augmented generation. In plain words, the AI first looks up relevant material in a trusted source, and then uses what it found when it works on an answer. Here that source is LOINC, so the suggestions start from the standard itself.

What the chapter covers

As the title says, the chapter looks at two sides of the work. One is how this approach was put into practice in LARA. The other is evaluation by experts. That second side matters to me because a tool that suggests codes has to be looked at by people who know what a good mapping is.

If you want the details of the work and of the evaluation, the chapter is the place to read them.
