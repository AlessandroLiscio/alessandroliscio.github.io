Master of Science - MS, Artificial Intelligence (110/110). Thesis: A Transformer-Based Application for Researchers Recommendation

MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Bologna, Sep 2019 to Mar 2022, with a thesis in natural language processing

I studied for an MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Bologna from September 2019 to March 2022, and graduated with 110 out of 110. My thesis was in natural language processing, the part of AI that works with human language. Its title was "A Transformer-Based Application for Researchers Recommendation", and it came out of an internship.

The idea

The question was how to find researchers who are similar to each other, so that one can be recommended to someone interested in another. Earlier work usually looked at connections: who writes papers with whom, and who cites whom. I looked at what researchers write.

I used language models to turn the title and abstract of each paper into a list of numbers that captures what the text is about. Papers on related topics should end up close to each other. To describe a researcher, I took the average of their papers. Finding similar researchers then meant looking for the ones closest to them.

What I worked with

I used a public collection of scientific papers from arXiv, and two existing models, without training them further on this data. To check how good the descriptions were, I tested whether they could be used to tell which subject categories a paper belonged to. I also drew them in two dimensions, to look at them.

Time and hardware were limited, so the thesis focused more on the quality of these descriptions than on the recommendations themselves. One of the two models proved very effective at describing papers from just their titles and abstracts. The natural next steps were to use the full text of the papers and to test the recommendations more.
