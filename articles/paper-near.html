A 2024 journal paper in Artificial Intelligence in Medicine on turning complex models into smaller, explainable decision support

The paper

One of my publications appeared in 2024 in the journal Artificial Intelligence in Medicine. Its full title is "An innovative artificial intelligence-based method to compress complex models into explainable, model-agnostic and reduced decision support systems with application to healthcare", and the short name is NEAR.

The idea in plain words

The title is long, but the idea it names can be said simply: start from a complex model and compress it into a smaller decision support system that can be explained.

A decision support system is a tool that helps a person make a choice, while the choice stays with that person. In healthcare, that person could be a clinician.

Each of the other words in the title adds something. "Compress" and "reduced" are about making things smaller. "Explainable" means a person should be able to follow why the system suggests what it does. "Model-agnostic" means the method is not tied to one particular kind of model.

Why these words matter to me

As I see it, a suggestion is hard to weigh when nobody can say where it comes from. In a setting like healthcare, I think the person reading it should have a way to question it. A smaller system with reasons you can follow is a good direction for that.

This page is only a short introduction to the idea. For the method itself and what it can and cannot do, the published paper is the place to look.
